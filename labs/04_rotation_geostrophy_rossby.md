# Lab 04 - Rotation Changes Everything: Geostrophic Adjustment and Rossby Waves

**Based on:** `10_geostrophic_adjustment.ipynb and 11_Rossby_wave_propagation.ipynb`

## Story

On a rotating planet, water does not simply flow straight downhill. It can adjust into a sideways balance and form slow planetary waves.

## Learning goals

- Coriolis force turns moving water.
- Geostrophic balance links pressure gradients and sideways flow.
- The deformation radius sets an important horizontal scale.
- Rossby waves are slow and often drift westward.

## Guiding questions

- What happens to a sea-surface bump on a non-rotating planet?
- What changes when rotation is included?
- How does beta make the pattern drift?

## Suggested experiment

1. Run adjustment from rest on an f-plane.
2. Start from a partially balanced initial condition.
3. Change the bump radius relative to the deformation radius.
4. Run the same case on a beta-plane and follow the drift.
