# Lab 03 - Wind, Storms, and Coastal Sea Level

**Based on:** `01_wind_gyre.ipynb, 08_storm_surge.ipynb, and 09_wind_driven_kelvin_wave.ipynb`

## Story

Wind can push water across the ocean, pile it against a coast during a storm, and launch sea-level signals that travel along boundaries.

## Learning goals

- Wind stress creates currents and sea-level slopes.
- Storm surge depends on wind, pressure, geometry, and rotation.
- Coastal Kelvin waves carry signals along coastlines.

## Guiding questions

- Where does wind pile up water?
- How does rotation change the coastal response?
- How fast does the coastal signal travel?

## Suggested experiment

1. Run a wind-driven gyre.
2. Run a moving storm and inspect the coastal sea level.
3. Switch rotation on and off.
4. Generate a wind-driven Kelvin wave along a boundary.

Keep the first run small enough to execute quickly. For outreach use, emphasize the animation first and the equations second. For older students, ask for one measured quantity and one comparison with theory.
