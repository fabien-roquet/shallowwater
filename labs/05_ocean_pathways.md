# Lab 05 - Ocean Pathways: Gyres, Tides, and Abyssal Flow

**Based on:** `01_wind_gyre.ipynb, 04_tides.ipynb, 05_abyssal_flow.ipynb, and 12_wind_gyre_reduced_gravity.ipynb`

## Story

Large-scale ocean pathways can be shaped by wind, tides, sources and sinks, and density-layer effects. This lab compares several ways to make water move.

## Learning goals

- Different forcings create different circulation patterns.
- Boundary currents are a natural result of rotation and friction.
- Tidal forcing oscillates rather than steadily pushing water one way.
- Source-sink forcing can mimic deep ocean renewal.

## Guiding questions

- How does a wind gyre differ from a tide?
- Where are the strongest currents?
- What happens when water is added in one region and removed elsewhere?

## Suggested experiment

1. Run the wind gyre case and identify the western boundary current.
2. Run the tide case and compare phases.
3. Run the abyssal source-sink case.
4. Optional: compare the barotropic gyre with a reduced-gravity gyre.
